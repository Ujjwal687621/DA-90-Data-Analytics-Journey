## Objective

Build a Product Performance analysis page in Power BI to identify:

- Which products generate the most revenue
- Which products generate the least revenue

- Which categories contain the highest-revenue products

---

## Business Question

> Which products drive revenue, which products underperform, where is revenue concentrated, and what should management investigate?

---

## Product Revenue Ranking

Created a Power BI measure using `RANKX()` to rank products by total revenue.

### DAX Measure

```DAX
Product Revenue Rank =
IF(
    ISINSCOPE(SalesTable[product_name]),
    RANKX(
        ALL(SalesTable[product_name]),
        [Total Revenue],
        ,
        DESC,
        DENSE
    )
)
```

The `ISINSCOPE()` condition prevents the Total row from displaying a misleading product rank.

Ranking QA:

- Revenue: **$34,456.73**
- Lowest-ranked product: **USB-C Cable**
- Rank: **25**
- Revenue: **$362.04**
- Total products ranked: **25**

### Top 5 Revenue Share

Created a DAX measure to calculate the Top 5 products' share of total revenue.

```DAX
Top 5 Revenue % =
DIVIDE(
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Revenue],
        TOPN(
            5,
            ALL(SalesTable[product_name]),
            [Total Revenue],
            DESC
        )
    ),
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Revenue],
        ALL(SalesTable[product_name])
    )
)
```

Result:

**51.62%**

The Top 5 products generated more than half of total revenue.

## Category Analysis

The five highest-revenue products came from two categories.

### Networking

- Mesh Wifi System
- Ethernet Adapter
- Wi-Fi Extender

Category revenue:

**$93.47K**

### Computer

- Desktop Computer
- Workstation

Category revenue:

**$61.68K**

Combined Computer and Networking revenue:

**$155.15K**

Total revenue:

**$224.87K**

Created a DAX measure to calculate the combined category share while explicitly removing the existing category filter context.

```DAX
Computer + Networking Revenue % =
DIVIDE(
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Revenue],
        REMOVEFILTERS(SalesTable[category]),
        SalesTable[category] IN {"Computers", "Networking"}
    ),
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Revenue],
        REMOVEFILTERS(SalesTable[category])
    )
)
```

Result:

**68.98%**

## Management Investigation Point

> Management should prioritize investigating revenue concentration in a small group of products and categories to understand the factors driving this concentration and assess potential risks from reliance on a limited product mix.

Potential areas for investigation include:

- Product pricing
- Unexpected price increases
- Promotions and discounts
- Product availability
- Customer demand
- Inventory levels
- Stockout history

These are investigation areas rather than conclusions about the causes of the observed revenue concentration.

---

## Data Limitation

The current dataset does not contain:

- Historical inventory levels
- Stockout dates
- Historical pricing changes
- Promotion/discount history
- Product cost or margin information
- Customer-level purchase information

Therefore, the analysis identifies revenue patterns but cannot determine the underlying cause of those patterns.

## DAX and Analytical Concepts Practiced

### RANKX

Used `RANKX()` to rank products by revenue.

### ALL

Used `ALL(SalesTable[product_name])` to remove the product filter context when calculating Top 5 revenue and product ranking.

### TOPN

Used `TOPN()` to dynamically identify the five highest-revenue products.

### REMOVEFILTERS

Used `REMOVEFILTERS(SalesTable[category])` to prevent the current category filter context from causing the Computer + Networking percentage measure to return 100%.

### ISINSCOPE

Used `ISINSCOPE()` to prevent the Total row from displaying a misleading product rank.

### DIVIDE

Used `DIVIDE()` for percentage calculations while following proper DAX division practices.

## Filter Context Troubleshooting

Two important DAX filter-context issues were identified during Day 42.

### Top 5 Revenue Share

The first version of the measure returned 100% because the denominator inherited the Top 5 product filter.

The denominator was corrected by removing the product filter:

```DAX
CALCULATE(
    [Total Revenue],
    ALL(SalesTable[product_name])
)
```

Correct result:

**51.62%**

### Computer + Networking Revenue Share

The first version returned 100% because the denominator inherited the category filter.

The measure was corrected using:

```DAX
REMOVEFILTERS(SalesTable[category])
```

Correct result:

**68.98%**

These troubleshooting steps reinforced the importance of understanding filter context in DAX.
