## 5. Fact vs. Hypothesis vs. Recommendation

A major lesson from Day 27 was learning to distinguish between what the data proves, what the analyst believes may be happening, and what action should be taken.

### Facts

- August had fewer transactions than July.
- Transactions declined by 40.98%.
- Units sold declined by 38.73%.
- Revenue declined by 25.66%.
- Revenue per transaction increased by 25.97%.
- Revenue per unit increased by 21.32%.
- Computer and Networking were the primary contributors to the revenue decline.
- Mesh Wi-Fi System had the largest individual product revenue decline.
- Office was the only category with positive revenue growth.

### Hypotheses

The following explanations were identified as possibilities but could not be confirmed using the current dataset:

- Customers may have purchased fewer high-value Computer and Networking products.
- Customers may have shifted purchasing behavior between Networking products.
- Customers may have delayed purchases because of upcoming promotions or discounts.
- Pricing may have influenced purchasing behavior.
- Changes in customer behavior may have contributed to the decline in transactions.
- Product availability may have affected sales.

### Recommendations

Recommendations were kept separate from factual observations.

For example:

**Fact:**  
Mesh Wi-Fi System units declined from 25 to 7.

**Hypothesis:**  
The decline could potentially be related to pricing, availability, promotions, customer behavior, or other market factors.

**Recommendation:**  
Conduct additional investigation and competitive pricing analysis before making a pricing decision.

## 6. Investigation Priorities

The following three priorities were identified for management:

### Priority 1 — Mesh Wi-Fi System

Investigate the 18-unit decline in Mesh Wi-Fi System sales because the product generated the largest individual revenue decline of $8,496.18.

### Priority 2 — Computer & Networking Performance

Investigate the broader decline across Computer and Networking because these categories collectively accounted for $13,282.99, or approximately 90.44%, of the net revenue decline.

### Priority 3 — Transaction Volume & Mix

Investigate why transactions declined by 40.98% while revenue per transaction increased by 25.97%, including potential effects from product mix, inventory availability, promotions, pricing, and customer behavior.

---

## 7. Data Gaps Identified

The current dataset is useful for identifying patterns but does not contain enough information to establish the underlying causes of the decline.

Additional data requested includes:

1. Historical inventory levels and stockout dates by product and warehouse.
2. Customer-level purchase history to analyze customer behavior and purchasing changes.
3. Promotion and campaign history by product, category, and date.
4. Upcoming or scheduled promotions and discounts to investigate potential purchase delays.
5. Actual selling price and discount history for individual transactions.
6. Website/store traffic data to evaluate changes in customer activity and conversion.
7. Competitor pricing and promotion information to evaluate market competitiveness.
8. Product cost and margin information to evaluate the profitability of potential pricing changes.

---

## 8. Business Recommendation — Mesh Wi-Fi System

A potential action discussed was temporarily reducing the price of the Mesh Wi-Fi System.

However, the recommendation was refined to avoid assuming that price caused the decline.

Recommended approach:

Conduct a competitive pricing analysis for the Mesh Wi-Fi System and, if Ujjsha's price is materially higher than comparable competitors, consider a temporary targeted price reduction or promotional offer.

The result should then be measured to determine whether the pricing change improves sales volume and financial performance.

The analysis should also consider profitability because increased revenue does not necessarily mean increased profit.

## 9. Pricing Experiment Framework

The potential pricing experiment was structured as:

**Competitor Price**
→
**Pricing Decision**
→
**Transaction Volume**
→
**Revenue**
→
**Profit / Gross Margin**

The purpose is to determine whether a pricing change produces a meaningful improvement without sacrificing profitability.

---

## 10. Experiment Metrics

The following were identified as useful measurements:

### Benchmark

**Competitor Price**

Competitor pricing is an external benchmark rather than a direct KPI for the experiment.

### Primary KPIs

**Transaction Volume**

Measures whether the pricing change increases purchasing activity.

**Sales Revenue**

Measures whether increased sales volume translates into higher total revenue.

**Profit / Gross Margin**

Measures whether the pricing change creates profitable growth rather than simply increasing sales volume.

## 11. Executive Summary

Overall revenue declined 25.66% from July to August, accompanied by a 40.98% decrease in transactions and a 38.73% decline in units sold. Despite the decline in overall volume, revenue per transaction increased 25.97% and revenue per unit increased 21.32%, indicating that the average value of transactions increased during August.

The revenue decline was primarily concentrated in the Computer and Networking categories, which collectively declined by $13,282.99 and accounted for approximately 90.44% of the net revenue decline. At the individual product level, Mesh Wi-Fi System experienced the largest revenue decline, decreasing by $8,496.18 as units sold fell from 25 to 7. The Office category was the only category to increase revenue, growing by $2,304.47 and partially offsetting declines elsewhere.

Management should prioritize investigating the decline in Mesh Wi-Fi System sales, the broader deterioration across Computer and Networking, and the 40.98% decline in transaction volume despite the 25.97% increase in revenue per transaction. Potential factors to investigate include product mix, inventory availability, promotions, pricing, and customer purchasing behavior.

Additional data would be required to determine the underlying causes, including historical inventory levels and stockout dates by product and warehouse; customer-level purchase history; promotion and campaign history; upcoming or scheduled promotions and discounts; actual selling-price and discount history; traffic data; competitor pricing; and product cost/margin information.

## 12. Key Learning from Day 27

The most important lesson from Day 27 was that a Data Analyst should not automatically treat a correlation as a cause.

The analytical process should be:

**Observed Fact**
→
**Potential Explanation**
→
**Additional Evidence Required**
→
**Validated Finding**
→
**Business Recommendation**
→
**Measurement**

The current dataset allows us to identify what happened, but additional data is required to confidently determine why it happened.

Another important lesson was that revenue should not be evaluated in isolation. Transaction volume, revenue per transaction, revenue per unit, and profitability provide additional context for understanding business performance.
