# Revenue Drivers & Business Investigation

**Project:** Ujjsha Retail — Portfolio Project #1  
**Tool:** Power BI  
**Analysis Period:** July–August

---

## Business Question

> What product and category factors drove the July-to-August revenue decline, and what should management investigate?

---

## Executive Finding

August revenue declined by **$14.69K** compared with July.

The Computer and Networking categories accounted for **90.44% of the net decline**, with:

- Computer: **46.21%**
- Networking: **44.23%**

At the product level, the largest declines within these categories came from:

- Mesh Wi-Fi System: approximately **-$8.5K**
- Desktop Computer: approximately **-$6.9K**

Several products partially offset the decline, including:

- Wi-Fi Extender: approximately **+$4.2K**
- Workstation: approximately **+$1.4K**
- Mini PC: approximately **+$0.8K**
- Ethernet Adapter: approximately **+$0.5K**

---

## Overall Revenue Change

August revenue change compared with July:

**-$14.69K**

This establishes the overall business movement that the category and product analysis investigates.

---

## Category Revenue Change

The July-to-August category changes were:

- Computer: approximately **-$6.8K**
- Networking: approximately **-$6.5K**
- Accessories: approximately **-$2.6K**
- Audio: approximately **-$1.2K**
- Office: approximately **+$2.3K**

The Office category increased revenue and partially offset declines in the other categories.

---

## Contribution to August Revenue Change

Category contribution to the overall July-to-August revenue change:

- Computer: **46.21%**
- Networking: **44.23%**
- Accessories: **17.40%**
- Audio: **7.85%**
- Office: **-15.69%**

Computer and Networking together contributed:

**90.44%**

of the net August revenue decline.

The contribution percentages do not sum to 100% because Office increased revenue and therefore offset part of the decline.

---

## Product-Level Drivers

The product-level investigation focused on the Computer and Networking categories.

The largest product-level declines were:

- Mesh Wi-Fi System: approximately **-$8.5K**
- Desktop Computer: approximately **-$6.9K**
- Wi-Fi Router: approximately **-$1.6K**
- Gaming Laptop: approximately **-$1.1K**
- Network Switch: approximately **-$1.0K**
- Business Laptop: approximately **-$1.0K**

Products showing growth included:

- Wi-Fi Extender: approximately **+$4.2K**
- Workstation: approximately **+$1.4K**
- Mini PC: approximately **+$0.8K**
- Ethernet Adapter: approximately **+$0.5K**

The product analysis identifies where the largest movements occurred but does not establish the underlying causes.

---

## Management Investigation

Management should investigate the decline in the Mesh Wi-Fi System and Desktop Computer, which were the two largest product-level declines within the Computer and Networking categories.

Potential areas for investigation include:

- Product availability
- Stockouts
- Historical pricing
- Promotions and discounts
- Changes in customer demand
- Competitive pricing

Additional historical data would be required to determine which factors contributed to the observed decline.

---

## Data Limitations

The current dataset does not contain sufficient information to determine the underlying causes of the observed revenue changes.

Additional data that would support further investigation includes:

- Historical inventory levels
- Stockout dates
- Historical pricing and discount data
- Promotion and campaign history
- Customer-level purchase history
- Competitor pricing
- Website or store traffic
- Product cost and margin information

The analysis identifies **where revenue changed**, but does not establish **why the change occurred**.

---

## Analytical Approach

The analysis followed the sequence:

**Overall Revenue Change → Category Change → Contribution to Change → Product-Level Drivers → Business Investigation → Additional Data Requirements**

This approach separates observed data patterns from potential explanations that require additional evidence.

---

## Key Analyst Takeaway

The key lesson from this analysis is that identifying a revenue decline is only the first step.

A strong business analysis should continue by determining:

- Where the change occurred
- Which categories contributed to it
- Which products drove the movement
- Which products offset the movement
- What additional evidence is needed to explain the cause

The available data supports the identification of revenue patterns but does not support causal conclusions without additional operational, pricing, customer, inventory, and competitive data.
